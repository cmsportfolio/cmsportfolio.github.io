About

Who I am

I am a Materials Scientist from Dhaka, Bangladesh, specializing in computational materials research at the intersection of first-principles theory and device-level simulation. I earned my B.Sc. in Materials Science and Engineering from Khulna University of Engineering & Technology (KUET) in 2025, where my research at the Computational Materials Science (CMS) Laboratory led to five peer-reviewed journal publications focused on perovskite and antiperovskite materials for energy applications.

My research interests span computational materials discovery, energy materials, photovoltaics, solid-state electrolytes, and emerging two-dimensional optoelectronic systems. By combining quantum-mechanical simulations with device-level modeling, I aim to bridge the gap between fundamental material properties and real-world technological performance.

My long-term research vision is to accelerate the discovery and design of next-generation materials for sustainable and futuristic technologies. I am particularly interested in developing advanced computational frameworks that enable the prediction and optimization of novel materials for energy conversion, energy storage, and electronic applications.

Currently, I am pursuing opportunities for Ph.D. research in Materials Science and Engineering and actively welcome academic and research collaborations. Looking ahead, I aspire to build a world-class Computational Materials Science Laboratory, contribute to the discovery of transformative materials, and establish a career in academia as a researcher, postdoctoral scholar, and faculty member dedicated to advancing materials innovation for the future.

Featured ongoing project · DFT + ML + SCAPS-1D

Interpretable Machine Learning and Active-Learning Optimization of Perovskite Solar Cells across the Full SCAPS-1D Device Parameter Space

Perovskite solar cells have reached certified efficiencies above 26% within little more than a decade, yet their performance is governed by a high-dimensional space of coupled parameters: the thicknesses, band gaps, electron affinities, carrier mobilities, doping levels, and defect densities of the transport and absorber layers, together with interface trap densities, contact work functions, temperature, and parasitic resistances. Physics-based simulators such as SCAPS-1D capture these interactions faithfully, but exhaustive exploration is computationally intractable — a coarse grid of ten values per parameter across a 26-parameter device implies 10²⁶ simulations — while conventional one-variable-at-a-time sweeps overlook the parameter couplings that dominate real device behavior.

This project develops a closed-loop, data-driven framework that overcomes this barrier. A Latin-hypercube-sampled SCAPS-1D dataset trains a gradient-boosted surrogate model that reproduces simulated Voc, Jsc, FF, and PCE at millisecond cost; SHAP analysis converts the model into ranked, directional design rules; and an active-learning loop screens millions of virtual candidates, verifies only the most promising in the simulator, and retrains on the results. The framework autonomously recovers known device physics — optimal band alignment, defect-density thresholds, and contact-barrier limits — and identifies device configurations unreachable by random sampling at a small fraction of brute-force cost, providing a transferable methodology for simulation-based photovoltaic design.
